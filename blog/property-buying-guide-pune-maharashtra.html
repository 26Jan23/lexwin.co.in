Most people buy property once or twice in a lifetime. A business acquires or leases premises far more often — but rarely with a team that has done it before, because the person handling it this time is usually handling it for the first time too. That mismatch between how often the decision gets made and how much is riding on it is exactly why property transactions go wrong more often than almost any other kind of contract.

This guide is written for that gap. It is not specific to one kind of buyer or one kind of property — it is deliberately broad, because the questions a home buyer in Wakad should be asking are structurally the same questions a manufacturer evaluating a shed in Chakan should be asking, or a company shortlisting a corporate office in Kharadi, or a bidder considering a bank-auctioned property. The property differs. The legal discipline required does not.

What follows covers who you might be as a buyer, what you might be buying, how you might be acquiring it, what due diligence actually means in practice, and where a property lawyer changes the outcome — not just the paperwork. We have tried to make this exhaustive rather than quick, because a property decision is not one where "quick" tends to serve you well.

Who This Guide Is For — Four Kinds of Property Buyers

"Property buyer" is not one category of person. The legal issues, the documentation, and the risks that matter shift meaningfully depending on who is buying and why. Before getting into property types and acquisition modes, it helps to see where you fit.

🏠

The Home Buyer

Buying a flat, bungalow, row house or plot for personal residence — first purchase, upgrade, or investment. Usually the highest emotional stake and the lowest legal familiarity, which is exactly the combination that gets exploited by poor documentation.

🏢

The Commercial Buyer

Buying or leasing retail space, an office unit, a showroom, or a mixed-use commercial premises — either as an end user running a business from it, or as an investor buying for rental yield.

🏭

The Industrial Buyer

Acquiring or leasing a shed, warehouse, factory unit, or industrial plot — typically a manufacturer, logistics operator, or industrial investor, often dealing with MIDC land, NA land, or built-to-suit development.

💼

The Corporate/Institutional Buyer

A company acquiring premises for its own corporate office, a co-working operator taking large floor plates, or an institutional investor building a real estate portfolio — decisions usually run through a legal and finance team rather than an individual.

The rest of this guide applies to all four — but wherever the legal treatment genuinely diverges by buyer type, we have called it out specifically rather than pretending one checklist fits everyone equally.

A Note on Farm Houses and Agricultural Land

Maharashtra restricts the purchase of agricultural land by persons who are not agriculturists, and separately regulates land ceiling and conversion to non-agricultural (NA) use. A "farm house" advertisement often understates how much of this framework still applies. Before treating agricultural land as a straightforward weekend-home purchase, a buyer needs clarity on the seller's title, NA conversion status (if any), and whether the buyer is even legally eligible to hold that land in that state.

Four Ways to Acquire Property — and Why the Choice Isn't Just Financial

Buyers tend to treat "purchase versus lease versus rent" as a financial decision alone — capital availability, tax treatment, balance sheet impact. Those factors matter, but each mode also carries a distinct legal instrument, a distinct registration requirement, and a distinct risk allocation between the two parties. Choosing the wrong instrument for the right commercial deal is a common and avoidable mistake.

Acquisition Mode Legal Instrument & Key Feature What Buyers Commonly Get Wrong
Outright Purchase Sale deed / conveyance deed, compulsorily registered under the Registration Act 1908; stamp duty payable on market value or agreement value, whichever is higher Treating the registered sale deed as proof of clear title — registration confirms the transaction happened, not that the seller's title was valid
Lease Lease deed; compulsory registration if the term exceeds 11 months (or in most Maharashtra formulations, one year), with stamp duty calculated on average annual rent plus premium, if any Signing long-term leases on unregistered documents to "save" stamp duty — this often makes the lease legally unenforceable against third parties and weak in a dispute
Rent / Leave and License Leave and license agreement, distinct from a lease under Maharashtra law; registration is mandatory regardless of tenure under the Maharashtra Rent Control framework and related state rules Using an unregistered or oral leave and license arrangement, which weakens the licensor's ability to recover possession and the licensee's ability to enforce agreed terms
Built-to-Suit (BTS) Development agreement plus lease/sale agreement, with detailed technical specifications, construction timelines, and penalty clauses annexed as binding schedules Treating the specification annexure as a formality rather than the enforceable core of the deal — vague specifications are the single biggest source of BTS disputes
❌ What Goes Wrong

A logistics company signs a 15-year leave and license agreement for a warehouse but leaves it unregistered to save on stamp duty and registration cost, and does not insist on a registered lease despite the long tenure. Three years in, the landlord sells the property and the new owner disputes the tenant's occupancy rights. Because the document was never registered, the tenant's position in court is materially weaker than it should have been for a fifteen-year commitment.

✓ What Protects the Buyer/Tenant

The same warehouse deal, structured correctly, is documented as a registered lease deed with the tenure, renewal terms, and exit conditions clearly stated, stamp duty paid on the correct basis, and a specific clause addressing what happens if the property changes hands during the lease term. When the landlord later sells, the new owner takes the property subject to the registered lease — and the tenant's occupancy is legally secure.

One related distinction worth flagging for anyone in Pune or Maharashtra dealing with residential or quasi-residential rental: a "lease" and a "leave and license" are not interchangeable terms, even though they get used loosely in conversation. A lease creates an interest in the property itself; a leave and license only creates a personal permission to occupy. Maharashtra's rent law framework treats them very differently, and using the wrong term in a document can genuinely change what rights the parties end up with — not just how the paperwork reads. What Every Buyer Must Check Before Signing Anything

Regardless of what you are buying or how you are acquiring it, a core set of checks applies across almost every property transaction in Maharashtra. This is the baseline — property-specific and mode-specific additions come later in this guide.

Title and Ownership Chain

Confirm that the person selling, leasing, or licensing the property actually has the legal right to do so, and that this right traces back through a clean, unbroken chain of ownership. A single unresolved link — an unregistered gift, a missing heir's signature, an unrecorded partition — can undermine everything built on top of it.

Land Records — 7/12 Extract, Property Card, and Index II

In Maharashtra, the 7/12 extract (Satbara Utara) governs agricultural and semi-urban land, while the Property Card (PR Card) governs land within municipal limits. Both show the current recorded owner, the nature of the land, and any encumbrances noted against it. Index II, obtained from the Sub-Registrar's office, shows the history of registered transactions on the property — a critical cross-check against what the seller tells you. Read our detailed guide to reading a 7/12 extract →

RERA Registration, Where Applicable

For most residential and many commercial projects, MahaRERA registration is mandatory before a developer can sell, market, or accept payments. Verify the registration number directly on the MahaRERA portal and cross-check the promised possession date, the sanctioned layout, and any recorded complaints or litigation against the project.

Encumbrances, Dues, and Litigation

An Encumbrance Certificate (or its Maharashtra equivalent search at the Sub-Registrar's office) shows whether the property is mortgaged, attached, or subject to any registered charge. Separately, check for pending property tax, society maintenance dues, electricity dues, and any ongoing civil or revenue litigation connected to the property — these do not always show up in a standard title search and often require a targeted court and revenue-record search.

Land Use, Zoning, and FSI

Confirm that the intended use — residential, commercial, industrial — matches the land's sanctioned use under the applicable Development Plan or Regional Plan. A property that looks and functions like a commercial unit but sits on land not zoned for commercial use creates a compliance problem that surfaces at the worst possible time, often during a regulatory inspection or a future resale.

Occupation Certificate and Completion Certificate

For any constructed property, the Occupation Certificate (OC) confirms the building was completed according to sanctioned plans and is legally fit for occupation. Buying — or lending against — a property without a valid OC is a common and underappreciated risk, particularly in resale transactions where the buyer assumes possession alone implies legality.

Stamp Duty, Registration, and GST

Stamp duty and registration charges in Maharashtra vary by transaction type, location, and — for certain categories — buyer profile. Commercial and industrial purchases may also carry GST implications that residential purchases typically do not. Get the tax treatment confirmed before you commit financially, not after.

For Companies and Institutional Buyers

If the buyer is a company, add board or shareholder authorisation, FEMA compliance for any foreign-linked entity, and internal delegation of signing authority to this checklist. A property transaction signed by someone without proper corporate authority can be challenged later — an avoidable risk that has nothing to do with the property itself.

Legal Due Diligence & Title Verification — The Core of Every Safe Purchase

Every section above eventually points back to one activity: legal due diligence. It is worth being precise about what that phrase actually means, because "due diligence" gets used loosely enough that buyers sometimes believe they have done it when they have only skimmed the surface.

Legal due diligence on property is the systematic verification of three things: that the seller genuinely owns what they are selling, that the property is free of undisclosed encumbrances or claims, and that the transaction — once completed — will actually give the buyer the rights they believe they are paying for. It is not a single document check. It is a process.

Title Search

Trace ownership back 30+ years through sale deeds, gift deeds, and court orders

Mutation & Revenue Records

Confirm the 7/12 extract or Property Card actually reflects the registered transfer

Litigation & Encumbrance

Search court and Sub-Registrar records for pending suits or registered charges

Physical Verification

Confirm boundaries, area, and access match what's on paper

What a Proper Title Search Involves

A title search traces ownership of the property backwards through the chain of title — typically 30 years in Maharashtra practice, and further where the history is complex or the property has passed through inheritance, partition, or multiple resales. It involves examining registered sale deeds, gift deeds, partition deeds, wills and succession documents, mortgage and release deeds, and court orders affecting the property, cross-referenced against the Index II records at the Sub-Registrar's office and the current land record entry.

Mutation Entries and Revenue Records

A registered sale deed transfers legal title, but the mutation entry in the revenue record (7/12 extract or Property Card) is what reflects that transfer in the government's own records. Gaps between registered transactions and mutation entries are common in Maharashtra and are exactly the kind of discrepancy a careful title search is built to catch — because an unmutated record can complicate everything from a future sale to a bank loan.

Litigation and Encumbrance Search

Beyond the encumbrance certificate, a thorough due diligence exercise includes a search of civil court records, revenue tribunal records, and — where relevant — cooperative court records for any pending or past litigation connected to the property or its previous owners. A property can look clean on paper and still be the subject of an active suit that has not yet been reflected in the land record.

Physical and Boundary Verification

Legal due diligence is not purely a documentary exercise. Confirming that the physical boundaries, area, and access match what is recorded on paper — and what is being sold to you — prevents a specific and surprisingly common dispute: paying for an area or access right that does not actually correspond to what exists on the ground.

Due Diligence by Property Type — What Changes Depending on What You're Buying

The core due diligence discipline above applies universally. But each property type layers on additional, specific checks that a generic checklist will miss.

Property TypeAdditional Checks Beyond Core Due Diligence
Home / Apartment MahaRERA registration and project status; society formation and conveyance status for older buildings; NOC from existing society for resale flats; verify actual carpet area against sanctioned plans; check for any pending society or municipal dues attached to the flat, not just the seller.
Farm House / Agricultural Land Confirm NA (non-agricultural) conversion order if the land is to be used for construction; verify buyer's eligibility to hold agricultural land under Maharashtra's agriculturist restrictions; check land ceiling compliance; verify tenancy records — protected tenants under Maharashtra tenancy law can complicate a seemingly clear title.
Industrial Shed / Warehouse Nature of land tenure — MIDC leasehold, freehold, or private industrial land, each with different transfer conditions; consent from MIDC or the relevant authority for transfer or sub-lease; fire NOC, pollution control board consent (CTE/CTO), and factory licence status; confirm permitted industrial use classification matches your intended operations.
Corporate Office / Commercial Unit Occupation certificate specifically for commercial use, not merely a residential OC being repurposed; premises society or federation rules on commercial activity, signage, and common area usage; GST registration and input credit implications; verify parking allocation is legally attached to the unit, not merely promised verbally.
Open Land / Vacant Plot Development Plan or Regional Plan reservation status — confirm the plot is not reserved for a road, garden, school, or other public purpose; zone classification versus intended use; boundary demarcation through a licensed surveyor; check for any tenancy, encroachment, or adverse possession claims, which are more common on vacant land than built property.
Auction-Sale Property Verify the auction was conducted under proper SARFAESI Act or court process with valid notice to the borrower; confirm actual versus symbolic possession status — many SARFAESI sales transfer symbolic possession only, leaving the buyer to pursue physical possession separately; check for prior encumbrances that may survive the auction; confirm no pending challenge to the auction itself before paying the full price.
Auction Properties Deserve Extra Caution

Bank and NBFC auction sales are frequently marketed as bargains, and sometimes genuinely are — but they are also sold strictly "as is, where is, whatever there is, without recourse." The due diligence burden shifts almost entirely to the buyer, and possession is not always immediate or straightforward even after payment. Treat an auction property as requiring more legal scrutiny than a conventional purchase, not less, regardless of how attractive the price looks.

The Legal Instrument for Each Acquisition Mode — Getting the Document Right

Beyond due diligence on the property itself, the acquisition mode you choose has its own documentation discipline.

Outright Purchase

The sale deed must accurately capture the agreed consideration, the exact schedule of property, all rights being transferred (including parking, terrace, or common area rights where relevant), and any conditions precedent. Stamp duty is payable on the higher of the transaction value or the government-notified ready reckoner rate — undervaluing a transaction to save stamp duty creates its own legal exposure later.

Lease

A lease deed for any term exceeding the statutory threshold must be registered to be fully enforceable, particularly against a subsequent purchaser of the property. Key clauses to scrutinise include renewal rights, rent escalation formulas, lock-in periods, exit and termination conditions, and — critically for industrial and commercial tenants — the landlord's obligations regarding structural repairs and statutory compliances.

Rent / Leave and License

Registration is mandatory in Maharashtra for leave and license agreements regardless of duration. The document should clearly state that it creates a licence and not a tenancy, specify the licence fee and deposit terms, and set out the process for termination and handover — ambiguity here is one of the most litigated areas of Maharashtra property law.

Built-to-Suit (BTS)

A BTS deal typically combines a development agreement with a lease or sale agreement, and its enforceability depends heavily on the specification annexure — the detailed technical schedule describing what will actually be built. Buyers and tenants should insist on binding timelines, milestone-linked payments, penalty clauses for delay, and a clear mechanism for resolving specification disputes before construction begins, not after.

How a Property Lawyer Actually Helps — At Every Stage, Not Just at Signing

Buyers often think of a property lawyer as someone who reviews the sale deed shortly before signing. In practice, the highest-value legal involvement happens earlier and continues later — and this is where LexWin, as a property lawyer working across residential, commercial, industrial, and corporate transactions in Pune and across Maharashtra, is typically engaged.

1

Pre-Purchase Due Diligence

Before any money changes hands, a title search, encumbrance check, and litigation search establish whether the property is safe to pursue at all. This is the stage where most serious defects are caught — and caught while walking away is still a free option.

2

Deal Structuring

Choosing the right acquisition mode — outright purchase, lease, leave and license, or built-to-suit — based on the buyer's actual commercial objective, not just convention, and identifying the tax and compliance consequences of each option before commitment.

3

Drafting and Negotiation

Reviewing or drafting the sale agreement, lease deed, or licence agreement to reflect what was actually negotiated — not a generic template — and negotiating on the buyer's behalf where terms are one-sided, unclear, or inconsistent with what was verbally agreed.

4

Registration and Stamp Duty Compliance

Ensuring the document is stamped and registered correctly, at the correct value, in the correct jurisdiction — errors here can be expensive to correct after the fact and, in some cases, cannot be corrected at all.

5

Post-Purchase Follow-Through

Confirming mutation of the buyer's name in revenue records, assisting with society membership or conveyance where applicable, and remaining available if a dispute, dues demand, or third-party claim surfaces after possession.

Who Needs This — and When

Legal support in a property transaction is not just for large, complex deals. The scale of the risk is often unrelated to the size of the transaction — a first-time home buyer and a company signing a warehouse lease can both face the same category of exposure if the documentation is wrong.

First-Time Home Buyer Reliance on the builder's or broker's representations; limited familiarity with RERA and society formation process Before booking, when reviewing the agreement for sale, and before final payment and possession
Resale Flat Buyer Chain-of-title gaps, unresolved society dues, unmutated records from prior transactions Before paying token money — resale defects are far easier to walk away from than to fix afterward
Commercial Investor Zoning mismatch, tenancy and rental yield assumptions not legally verified, GST and tax treatment At the shortlisting stage, before commercial terms are finalised
Manufacturer / Industrial Buyer Land tenure restrictions, transfer consent requirements, environmental and fire compliance Before signing any MOU or letter of intent — transfer restrictions can affect deal feasibility itself
Corporate Real Estate Teams Corporate authorisation, multi-location lease consistency, exit and relocation flexibility As part of standard procurement process for any new premises, lease renewal, or relocation

Auction Bidders Limited recourse post-sale, uncertain possession timelines, potential prior encumbrances Before bidding, not after winning the auction — due diligence options narrow sharply once you've committed

Pre-Purchase Checklist — 15 Questions to Ask Before You Pay

Whatever you are buying, however you are acquiring it, run through this before any advance or token payment changes hands. If you cannot confidently answer most of these, that is the signal to bring in legal support before proceeding — not after.

  • Have I independently verified the seller's/landlord's title, rather than relying solely on what they or their broker told me?
  • Have I obtained and reviewed the current 7/12 extract or Property Card, not an outdated copy?
  • If applicable, have I verified the MahaRERA registration and checked for any recorded complaints against the project?
  • Have I checked for encumbrances, mortgages, or charges registered against the property?
  • Have I confirmed there is no pending litigation connected to the property or its prior owners?
  • Does the land-use classification match my intended use of the property?
  • For a constructed property, does a valid Occupation Certificate exist?
  • Have I confirmed there are no outstanding property tax, society, or utility dues attached to the property?
  • For agricultural or farm-house land, have I confirmed NA conversion status and my own eligibility to purchase?
  • For industrial land, have I confirmed transfer consent requirements and environmental/fire compliance?
  • Have I chosen the correct legal instrument — sale deed, lease deed, or leave and license — for what I am actually acquiring?
  • Is the document I am about to sign registered, or will it need to be, given its term and value?
  • Have I confirmed the correct stamp duty and registration charges applicable to this transaction?
  • For an auction property, have I confirmed the type of possession being transferred and the process to obtain physical possession?
  • Has this agreement been reviewed by a property lawyer before I sign or pay anything?

Frequently Asked Questions

Grouped by the kind of buyer and situation you are most likely to be in.

For Home Buyers

Is RERA registration mandatory for every flat I buy in Pune?
For most projects, yes. Under the RERA Act, any project with more than 500 square metres of land or more than eight units must be registered with MahaRERA before the developer markets, sells, or accepts payment. Always verify the registration number independently on the MahaRERA website rather than taking the developer's word for it.

What is the difference between an agreement for sale and a sale deed?
The agreement for sale is the contract that sets out the terms on which the developer or seller agrees to transfer the property once conditions (usually full payment) are met. The sale deed is the actual instrument of transfer, executed and registered once those conditions are satisfied. Possession and legal title generally follow the sale deed, not the agreement for sale alone.

Do I need a lawyer even if I am buying from a reputed builder?
A reputed builder reduces certain risks but does not remove the need for independent review. Sale agreements are drafted to protect the developer by default. A lawyer reviews the agreement for one-sided clauses, confirms the project's legal standing independently, and makes sure your specific interests — payment schedule, possession date, penalty for delay — are properly reflected.

What should I check specifically when buying a resale flat rather than a new one?
Trace the full chain of ownership since the property was first sold, confirm the current society has recorded the seller as a valid member with no pending dues, verify the flat has a valid Occupation Certificate, and check that all prior transactions were properly registered and mutated.

What happens if I skip the title search on a flat purchase because "it's just a flat, not land"?
A flat's title is only as sound as the land beneath it. If the underlying land carries a defective title, an unresolved tenancy, or an improperly converted agricultural classification, that defect does not disappear because a building was constructed on top of it — it can surface years later, often at resale or when applying for a loan against the property.

For Commercial & Corporate Office Buyers

What is the biggest legal difference between buying a commercial unit and a residential flat?
Land-use and occupation certificate classification. A unit can look identical to a residential flat but sit in a building or on land not sanctioned for commercial use, which creates compliance exposure regardless of how the space is being used in practice. GST treatment and premises society rules on commercial activity also differ meaningfully from residential norms.

Should our company buy or lease office premises?
This is primarily a commercial and financial decision, but the legal treatment differs: a purchase requires full title due diligence and a higher one-time compliance burden, while a lease requires careful negotiation of renewal, escalation, and exit terms, since the company does not control the underlying asset. Many growing companies deliberately choose leasing for flexibility during periods of uncertain headcount or expansion plans.

What corporate authorisations are needed before signing a property purchase or lease?
Typically a board resolution or equivalent internal approval authorising the transaction and naming the signatory, and — for larger transactions or foreign-linked entities — confirmation that the deal is compliant with any applicable FEMA or sectoral investment rules. Skipping this step can expose the transaction to challenge later, even if the property itself is entirely clean.

We are taking a large floor plate on lease — what should our lease deed specifically address?
Beyond standard terms, look closely at renewal rights and notice periods, rent escalation formula and frequency, lock-in period and early exit consequences, maintenance and common area charge structure, fit-out and de-fit-out obligations, and what happens to the lease if the landlord sells the building during the term.

For Industrial Buyers

What is different about buying or leasing MIDC land compared to private industrial land?
MIDC-allotted land is typically leasehold, not freehold, and carries specific conditions on transfer, sub-lease, and change of use that require MIDC's consent. Private industrial land may be freehold but still requires confirmation that its zoning genuinely permits the industrial activity you intend to carry out.

What environmental and regulatory clearances should we verify before taking an industrial shed?
Depending on the nature of the industry, this can include Pollution Control Board consent to establish and operate (CTE/CTO), fire NOC, factory licence, and — for certain categories — environmental clearance at the project level. Confirm which of these the existing occupant or landlord already holds and which will need to be newly obtained in your name.

Can we sub-lease or assign an industrial shed we've taken on lease?
Only if the underlying lease or allotment terms permit it, and often only with the landlord's or the allotting authority's prior written consent. This is a frequently overlooked clause that matters significantly if your business plans might change over the lease term.

What is a built-to-suit (BTS) arrangement and when does it make sense for an industrial buyer?
In a BTS deal, the developer constructs the facility to the buyer's or tenant's specifications, usually under a combined development and lease agreement. It suits manufacturers and logistics operators with specific structural, height, floor-load, or utility requirements that a standard shed would not meet — but it depends heavily on binding, detailed specifications and milestone-linked payment terms to work as intended.

For Auction Property Buyers

What does "as is, where is, whatever there is" mean in an auction sale?
It means the property is sold without any warranty from the selling bank or institution regarding its physical condition, occupancy status, or completeness of title. The buyer takes on substantially more due diligence responsibility than in a conventional sale, because there is limited recourse against the seller after the sale is complete.

If I buy a property at a bank auction, do I get immediate physical possession?
Not always. Many SARFAESI sales initially transfer only symbolic possession, particularly where the borrower or an occupant has not vacated. Obtaining actual physical possession can require a separate legal process, and buyers should factor this timeline and cost into their decision before bidding.

Can an auction sale be challenged after I've paid the full amount?
Yes, in certain circumstances — for instance, if the original borrower successfully challenges the auction process itself, or if procedural requirements under the SARFAESI Act were not properly followed. This is precisely why pre-bid legal verification of the auction process and property history matters, rather than treating the auction notice as sufficient assurance on its own.

Are there prior encumbrances I should worry about on an auctioned property?
Possibly. While a SARFAESI sale generally extinguishes the enforcing bank's own charge, other prior encumbrances, statutory dues, or third-party claims may not automatically disappear. A targeted encumbrance and dues search before bidding is essential, not optional.

On Legal Due Diligence & Title Verification

How far back should a title search go?
Standard Maharashtra practice looks at least 30 years of ownership history, and further where the chain includes inheritance, partition, or gift transactions, which can be harder to trace cleanly. The right depth depends on the specific property's history, not a fixed rule that applies identically everywhere.

What is the difference between a 7/12 extract and a Property Card?
The 7/12 extract applies to agricultural and semi-urban land and is maintained under the Maharashtra Land Revenue Code. The Property Card applies to land within municipal or urban limits and is maintained by the City Survey office. Knowing which record governs your property is the first step to reading it correctly.

What is an encumbrance certificate and why does it matter?
It is an official record of all registered transactions — mortgages, charges, liens — against a specific property over a chosen period. It is one of the clearest ways to confirm that a property is not already pledged, mortgaged, or subject to a registered claim you were not told about.

Can I do due diligence myself, or do I need a lawyer?
A motivated buyer can obtain some of these documents independently, but interpreting them correctly — spotting a break in the chain of title, recognising an inconsistency between the 7/12 extract and the seller's claims, or identifying a defect in an old partition deed — requires legal training most buyers do not have. The documents being publicly available is not the same as being able to read what they actually mean for your specific transaction.

On Pune & Maharashtra-Specific Rules

Do Pune and other Maharashtra cities have different stamp duty rates?
Stamp duty in Maharashtra is largely governed at the state level, though local body surcharges (such as those applicable within Pune Municipal Corporation limits) can affect the final payable amount. Rates and applicable surcharges are revised periodically, so always confirm the current applicable rate before budgeting for a transaction rather than relying on a figure from an earlier purchase or a general online source.

What is the "ready reckoner rate" and why does it matter for my purchase?
It is the government-notified minimum valuation for property in a given area, used as the floor for calculating stamp duty. If your actual transaction value is below the ready reckoner rate, stamp duty is still calculated on the higher ready reckoner value — which is worth knowing before you negotiate a price that assumes duty will be calculated on the lower figure.

I am an NRI buying property in Pune — is the process different for me?
The core due diligence process is the same, but NRIs typically need to navigate FEMA compliance, may execute documents through a registered power of attorney if unable to be physically present, and should pay particular attention to repatriation rules if they intend to eventually sell and remit proceeds abroad.

Can a non-agriculturist buy agricultural land in Maharashtra for a farm house?
This is restricted under Maharashtra law in several circumstances, and the position depends on factors including the buyer's occupation, the land's location, and whether NA conversion has been obtained or is intended. This is one of the areas where a blanket "yes, anyone can buy a farm house" assumption most often turns out to be incomplete — it needs to be checked against the specific facts before proceeding.
